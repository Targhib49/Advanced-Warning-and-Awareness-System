*AWAS — what we actually use (1/5)*
_Layer 1: driver monitoring_

*Face landmarks*
MediaPipe Face Landmarker (Tasks API) — 478 points per face, runs on CPU.
This IS a neural network, so we are already using deep learning even in the
"geometric" part. Important to say correctly.
Paper: Kartynnik et al. (2019), "Real-time Facial Surface Geometry from
Monocular Video on Mobile GPUs", arXiv:1907.06724

*Eye openness*
EAR (Eye Aspect Ratio) — a ratio between 6 landmarks per eye.
Paper: Soukupová & Čech (2016), "Real-Time Eye Blink Detection using Facial
Landmarks", CVWW
Note: their best result used a classifier over a window of EAR values, NOT a
bare threshold. Most tutorials copy the weaker version.

*Head pose*
solvePnP (OpenCV) against a generic 3D face model → pitch/yaw/roll.
Used to reject frames where head rotation would corrupt EAR.

*Our own addition — per-driver calibration*
We do NOT threshold EAR directly. We learn each driver's own open and closed
reference during known-alert periods, then score every frame on their personal
scale. No ML needed, just robust statistics.
Reason: baseline eye shape varies 2–3x between people, so one fixed threshold
is too sensitive for one driver and too deaf for the next.

*AWAS — what we actually use (2/5)*
_The drowsiness measure_

*PERCLOS*
The fraction of a 60-second window the eyes are more than 80% closed (P80).
This is the validated standard, not something we invented.
Paper: Dinges & Grace (1998), "PERCLOS: A Valid Psychophysiological Measure of
Alertness as Assessed by Psychomotor Vigilance", report FHWA-MCRT-98-006,
US Department of Transportation
Originally scored by humans watching video — which is why it is a sound
measure independently of any automatic detector.

Also from Wierwille and colleagues' driver-monitoring work at Virginia Tech in
the early 1990s, which is where PERCLOS originates.

*Blinks vs microsleeps*
We separate them by duration. A closure of 500 ms or more is a microsleep, not
a blink. 2 seconds is critical.

*Hysteresis*
A level must hold for 3 consecutive updates before escalating, 10 before
relaxing. Without this the level chatters across a threshold and the driver is
alerted, un-alerted and re-alerted within seconds.

*Ground truth*
KSS (Karolinska Sleepiness Scale), 1–9, asked at trip start and end.
Paper: Åkerstedt & Gillberg (1990), "Subjective and objective sleepiness in the
active individual", International Journal of Neuroscience 52(1–2), 29–37

*AWAS — what we actually use (3/5)*
_Machine learning_

*Model*
EyeStateNet — a small CNN, *23,538 parameters*, PyTorch.
Input: 32x32 grayscale eye crop. Output: probability the eye is closed.
Replaces the EAR threshold, which breaks under head rotation and varies by
person.

*Dataset*
MRL Eye Dataset — 84,898 infrared eye images, 37 subjects, 3 cameras.
Source: mrl.cs.vsb.cz/eyedataset (Media Research Lab, VSB Ostrava)
Free, no application needed. Labels are encoded in the filename: subject,
gender, glasses, eye state, reflections, lighting, sensor.

*The most important methodological point*
We split the data *by subject*, never by image. MRL has 37 people with hundreds
of near-identical photos each. A random split puts the same eyes in training
and testing and reports an accuracy that means nothing.
Concept to look up: *data leakage*.
This is the difference between a number a judge can attack and one they cannot.

*Baselines*
We report a majority-class baseline and a logistic regression on raw pixels
next to the CNN. If the CNN does not beat them, we ship the simpler model.
Deep learning that does not beat a boosted tree is a liability on an embedded
device, not an achievement.

*AWAS — what we actually use (4/5)*
_System, response and deployment_

*Decision logic*
A finite state machine — parked / moving / stopped at light / traffic jam /
shutdown. It is *pure*: no camera, no clock, no model inside it. That is why we
can test it exhaustively.

*Response*
Every line the system can say is written in advance and rendered to audio
before it runs. Nothing is generated while driving.
Measured: *25 ms* from decision to audible, against a 300 ms budget.
Rendering now uses macOS `say`; on the Pi it will use Piper (offline TTS).
Playback: sounddevice, pre-warmed stream, clips already in memory.

*Export for the Pi*
ONNX + ONNX Runtime, with int8 static quantization.
Honest finding: at 23.5k parameters the int8 model is actually *larger* than
fp32 (49 KB vs 25 KB) because the quantize/dequantize nodes cost more than the
weights save. It is ~40% faster but we will probably ship fp32.

*Logging*
JSONL, one record per event. Derived numbers only — no video, no images, no
landmark coordinates. Nothing in the log can reconstruct a picture of a driver.

*Target hardware*
Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB) with active cooling. Note the original BOM said Pi 4B and
had no IR illuminator — a NoIR camera with nothing emitting infrared cannot see
at night, which is half the use case.

Metal GPU delegate and crash on CPU), and numpy must be *below 2.0* (several
wheels are still built against 1.x).
